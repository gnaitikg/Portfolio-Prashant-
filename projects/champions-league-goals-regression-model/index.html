Champions League Goals: A Regression Model of What Drives Scoring

Which game statistics actually predict goals? We took 36 European teams from the Champions League and ran a backward-elimination regression. Only two variables survived: attempts on target and possession. The model explains over 70% of goals scored.

R² 0.72Share of goals explained by shots on target and possession, 36 teams

The full story

Every club wants more goals, but the useful question is which numbers actually drive them. Management and coaches have plenty of match statistics and little clarity on which ones to build tactics around.

We took a cross-section of 36 teams from the previous Champions League season and tested six performance variables: attempts on target, passes completed, possession, saves, tackles won and cards. We ran a regression, dropped the variable with the highest p-value, and repeated until two were left: attempts on target and possession.

The model is highly significant (R squared 0.72, adjusted 0.71, F-statistic 43). In this data, roughly every third attempt on target becomes a goal, and extra possession adds a smaller but real boost. The intercept is not meaningful in football terms, so we set it aside.

The recommendation: generate shots on target, and use possession productively rather than passively. Manchester City had more of the ball than Liverpool but scored fewer goals.

A small dataset, so a first model rather than a forecast, but a clear picture of what to optimise.

With Dayan, Maria Camila and 3 others. Prepared as part of the International MBA at IE Business School, Madrid.
