The full story

Oatly went from a niche Swedish health product to a global oat milk brand in under a decade, and its supply chain barely kept up. After the Great Oatly Shortage of 2017, COVID disruptions and a failed IPO expansion plan, the company sat between two supply chain models without committing to either.

We assessed where Oatly stands on the supply chain maturity curve and concluded it is stuck between Enterprise Integrated and Extended Supply Chain. Then we built a five-year roadmap to fix that.

The plan has three parts: decouple proprietary oat base production from partner facilities to protect the IP, implement a cloud ERP for real-time demand visibility, and shift from inside-out historical forecasting to outside-in demand sensing. Year three introduces modular manufacturing, a plug-and-play production unit that can move to new markets without rebuilding from scratch.

The goal was not to grow faster. It was to stop being reactive and start being resilient.

With Dayan, Ritvik Krishnan and 2 others. Prepared as part of the International MBA at IE Business School, Madrid.
